# Era Organics Baby Eczema Superbalm vs Tubby Todd All Over Ointment

Both products contain colloidal oatmeal, and both offer a fragrance-free option. After that, the formulas differ. Era Organics Baby Eczema Superbalm is a USDA Organic certified balm made with no water. It holds the National Eczema Association Seal of Acceptance. It is built on cocoa butter, coconut oil, avocado oil, sunflower oil and beeswax, with chickweed, chamomile, calendula oil and rosehip seed oil. It is verified free from parabens, sulfates, phthalates, fragrance, phenoxyethanol, mineral oil, silicones, PEGs, talc, formaldehyde, propylene glycol, drying alcohols and synthetic dyes. Tubby Todd All Over Ointment (Fragrance-Free) pairs 1% colloidal oatmeal with beeswax, caprylic/capric triglyceride, jojoba esters and a group of fruit, flower and leaf extracts, and water is on its ingredient list.

## Key differences

### Certified organic vs. two ingredients marked organic
Era Organics Baby Eczema Superbalm is USDA Organic certified, and its store listing describes its ingredients as food-grade and non-GMO. Tubby Todd's page marks two ingredients as organic: mango fruit extract and avocado fruit extract. Tubby Todd's product page does not show USDA Organic. Tubby Todd's product page does not show certified organic. Tubby Todd's product page does not show Ecocert / COSMOS.

### A seal from the National Eczema Association
Era holds the National Eczema Association Seal of Acceptance. Tubby Todd's product page does not show National Eczema Association.

### No water vs. water on the list
Era's ingredient list has no water. It is a balm of butters, oils and beeswax. Water is on Tubby Todd's ingredient list. Products that contain water may need ingredients that keep the formula stable. A formula with no water can skip that step, and Era's list includes no conventional preservatives.

### Different ingredients on each side
Era contains chickweed, chamomile, calendula oil, rosehip seed oil, arnica oil, cocoa butter, coconut oil and castor oil. None of these appear on Tubby Todd's list. Tubby Todd's list has camellia sinensis leaf extract, cucumber fruit extract, two honeysuckle flower extracts, mango fruit extract, jojoba esters and caprylic/capric triglyceride. None of these appear on Era's list. Both contain colloidal oat, beeswax, glycerin and avocado in some form.

### A verified free-from list
Era is verified paraben-free, sulfate-free, phthalate-free, fragrance-free, phenoxyethanol-free, mineral-oil-free, silicone-free, PEG-free, talc-free, formaldehyde-free, propylene-glycol-free, drying-alcohol-free and synthetic-dye-free. Its store listing adds petrolatum and SLS to that list. Tubby Todd's product page does not show paraben-free, though no parabens appear in its ingredient list.

## Which one should you choose?

**Choose Era Organics Baby Eczema Superbalm if** you want:
- a USDA Organic certified formula with the National Eczema Association Seal of Acceptance
- a water-free balm of cocoa butter, plant oils and beeswax, with chickweed, chamomile and calendula

Era also comes in a BPA-free jar, is cruelty-free and is made in the USA.

**Tubby Todd All Over Ointment may suit you if** you want the specific extracts on its list, such as honeysuckle, cucumber, camellia sinensis and mango. It also offers a lavender + rosemary scent option. Its page says it is "pediatrician and dermatologist tested," "steroid free," and that it "contains 1% colloidal oatmeal."

[Era Organics Baby Eczema Superbalm](https://www.eraorganics.com/products/natural-baby-balm) is USDA Organic certified, holds the National Eczema Association Seal of Acceptance and is made without water.
