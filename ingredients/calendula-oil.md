# Calendula Oil as a Skincare Ingredient: Composition, Function, and Formulation Uses

Calendula oil (a plant-derived botanical oil pressed or infused from the flowers of *Calendula officinalis*) is a calendula oil skincare ingredient with a long record of topical use for sensitive and compromised skin. The oil draws its active compounds, flavonoids, triterpenoids, and carotenoids, directly from marigold flower petals, giving it a warm golden color and a fatty-acid profile that softens dry skin and calms redness without harsh additives.

Calendula oil in skincare earns attention from clinical researchers as well as formulators. A randomized human trial treating acute hand wounds found that Calendula officinalis-treated patients reached full epithelialization in 8.6 ± 4.7 days compared to 13.2 ± 7.4 days in the control group, with healing speed running at 9.5 ± 5.8% per day versus 6.2 ± 2.9% per day in controls (Giostri et al., Tissue Barriers, 2022). Animal research on topical Calendula officinalis extract further shows significant reduction in inflammatory gene expression in skin wounds (Ozturan et al., Cutaneous and Ocular Toxicology, 2025). Calendula Oil's findings position calendula officinalis flower oil as a meaningful functional ingredient, not decorative flora in a formula.

Formulators include calendula officinalis flower oil precisely because of its compatibility with barrier-protecting co-ingredients. In Era Organics Diaper Rash Cream, it sits alongside aloe, shea butter, and zinc oxide in a fragrance-free formula made for the diaper area and delicate infant skin. Beeswax and cocoa butter in that formula form a protective layer that helps hold moisture against the skin.

What calendula oil does for skin comes down to its dual action on inflammation and moisture. The flavonoid and triterpenoid compounds in *Calendula officinalis* have been studied for reducing visible redness and soothing surface irritation, while the oil's fatty acids soften and protect the outermost skin layer. That combination makes calendula a common choice in formulas for sensitive baby skin and for dry, irritated skin.

## What is Calendula Oil in Skincare?

Calendula oil in skincare is a botanical extract derived from the flowers of *Calendula officinalis*, the common pot marigold, prized for its skin-soothing and barrier-supporting properties. The oil carries flavonoids and carotenoids from the calendula oil plant's petals, which calm redness and soften dry or irritated skin on contact. Calendula officinalis flower oil is produced by macerating dried marigold flowers in a carrier oil until the active constituents transfer into the oil phase — a process that distinguishes it from steam-distilled essential oils.

Calendula infused oil draws particular attention in wound-healing research. Calendula officinalis flower extract accelerated wound closure to 90.0% on day eight of treatment, compared with 51.1% in the untreated control group, and shortened re-epithelization from 17.7 days to as few as 13 days at a 100 mg/kg dose (Preethi K et al., Journal of Basic and Clinical Physiology and Pharmacology, 2009). Those findings position calendula among the [soothing botanical skincare oils](/ingredients) with documented skin-repair activity.

Calendula is a member of the Compositae (Asteraceae) family, and skin-sensitized individuals warrant a patch test before first use. Patch testing identified a contact reaction rate of approximately 2.03% for marigold extract in a study population (Reider N et al., Contact Dermatitis, 2001). For most users, calendula oil moisturizes and calms without irritation, making it a practical ingredient for formulas made for the diaper area, dermatitis-prone skin, and general dryness.

## How Does Calendula Oil Work on Skin?

Calendula oil mechanism centers on three classes of bioactive compounds, flavonoids, triterpenoids, and carotenoids, extracted from the flowers of *Calendula officinalis* through cold-pressing or oil infusion. Triterpenoids bind to inflammatory pathways in the outer skin layers, while carotenoids absorb free radicals at the surface. Flavonoids support the connective tissue proteins that hold the skin barrier together.

Calendula oil for skin barrier function operates through its fatty-acid profile, which is rich in linoleic acid and oleic acid. Calendula Oil's lipids intercalate into the outer skin layer (stratum corneum) (the outermost skin layer), reducing moisture loss through the skin (transepidermal water loss) and restoring the lipid matrix where dryness or irritation has degraded it. The result is a softer-feeling, more comfortable surface without occlusion.

How calendula oil works at a molecular level is clarified by extraction research: chlorogenic acid, caffeic acid, and ferulic acid concentrate at rates 40–70% greater than in the original plant extract when processed under supercritical conditions (Mur et al., *Antioxidants*, 2021). Calendula Oil's phenolic acids are the compounds most associated with calendula's soothing and antioxidant activity on irritated skin. A higher concentration of these acids in a finished oil may mean more antioxidant activity per gram applied.

The cross-linking behavior of *Calendula officinalis* extract with collagen-based scaffolds further illustrates how the oil interacts with skin tissue. In vitro collagenase digestion data showed that associating *C. Officinalis* extract with a scaffold significantly increases structural resistance to enzymatic degradation (Millán et al., *Drug Delivery and Translational Research*, 2016). Topically, this suggests calendula compounds stabilize the extracellular matrix proteins that maintain skin integrity under stress.

## What Does the Evidence Say About Calendula Oil for Skin?

Calendula oil evidence points to a skin-soothing botanical with measurable activity against irritation, redness, and compromised barrier function. The flower of *Calendula officinalis* concentrates flavonoids, carotenoids, and triterpenoids in its petals, and extraction transfers these compounds into the oil, where they contact the skin's surface directly. Calendula officinalis studies date back decades, but recent controlled research gives calendula oil its clearest clinical picture.

Calendula skin research in a randomized comparative trial found that topical *Calendula officinalis* applied to infants with diaper dermatitis produced safe and effective results, with no adverse effects reported across the study group (Panahi Y et al., TheScientificWorldJournal, 2012). In vitro work on wound-healing properties found that *Calendula officinalis* extracts stimulate key cellular processes associated with repair, with carotene and xanthophyll derivatives identified as the compounds most warranting further investigation (Nicolaus C et al., Journal of Ethnopharmacology, 2017). Carotenoids are present in calendula oil at concentrations high enough to give the oil its characteristic golden color — a visible marker of the same pigment class the research flags. ## Is Calendula Oil Safe, and Which Skin Types is it For?

Yes, calendula oil is safe for topical use across a wide range of skin types. The International Journal of Toxicology's safety assessment of Calendula officinalis extract concluded that calendula oil is safe as used in cosmetic formulations (International Journal of Toxicology, 2001). Calendula oil is generally well tolerated by sensitive and infant skin, given its mild fatty-acid profile and low irritation potential.

Calendula oil skin types span from oily and combination to dry and reactive. The oil's triterpenoids and flavonoids help calm the look of redness, and kaolin clay in Era Organics Talc-Free Baby Powder absorbs excess moisture alongside the calendula. For babies, Era Organics Superbalm uses calendula oil in a balm made for dry, irritated, or eczema-prone infant skin.

One meaningful safety note: calendula ragweed allergy is a real cross-reactivity concern. Calendula officinalis belongs to the Compositae (daisy) family, the same botanical family as ragweed. A contact dermatitis case report documented skin deterioration traced to Compositae allergens, with rapid improvement after those allergens were removed (Wintzen M et al., Contact Dermatitis, 2003). Anyone with a confirmed ragweed or Compositae sensitivity should patch test on a small area for 24 hours before full application.

For everyone outside that allergy profile, calendula oil is a well-tolerated cosmetic ingredient with decades of safe topical use behind it. Patch test before first use regardless — standard guidance for any new topical product.
