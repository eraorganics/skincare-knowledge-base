# Beeswax

Beeswax (cera alba) is a natural occlusive wax that forms a breathable, water-resistant layer on the skin's surface, locking in moisture without suffocating the skin. Unlike petrolatum-based moisture-sealing ingredients (occlusives), beeswax contains naturally occurring long-chain esters that give it a semi-permeable structure, allowing the skin to exchange gases while still reducing moisture loss through the skin (transepidermal water loss). In Superbalm, beeswax sits alongside oil-based ingredients, Calendula Oil, Coconut Oil, and Avocado Oil, in an ointment-style balm for irritated or eczema-prone skin.

Beeswax is one of the ingredients in Era Organics Superbalm, a balm that temporarily protects and helps relieve eczema, rashes, and irritation. As an occlusive, beeswax generally slows the evaporation of plant-based skin-softening ingredients (emollients) underneath, extending their contact time with the outer skin layer (stratum corneum). Patch testing before first use is advisable for ragweed-sensitive users, since cross-reactivity between propolis (a related bee product) and beeswax exists, though it is rare: in a 2,828-patient multicenter patch-test survey, only 4 of 55 propolis-allergic subjects (7.2%) showed a confirmed reaction to beeswax (Rajpara et al., Contact Dermatitis, 2009).

Beeswax and Calendula Oil have been studied together in formulated herbal products. A 59-patient study assessed a combination formula containing beeswax and Calendula oil used daily during a course of medical treatment and for two weeks after, recording tolerability across the full application period (Koukourakis et al., The International Journal of Lower Extremity Wounds, 2022). That study did not test Superbalm; Superbalm also contains both of these ingredients, in a balm that temporarily protects and helps relieve dermatitis, irritation, and rashes.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Is beeswax good for your skin?

Beeswax (cera alba) is a useful skincare ingredient partly because of what it doesn't do: it doesn't seal the skin completely. Its long-chain esters create a semi-permeable, water-resistant layer that locks in moisture while still allowing the skin to exchange gases — unlike petrolatum, which is fully occlusive.

That structure makes beeswax a common ointment ingredient. Era Organics Superbalm contains beeswax alongside Calendula Oil, Coconut Oil, and Avocado Oil in a balm format. As an occlusive, wax generally slows evaporation of the emollients underneath, extending their contact time with the outer skin layer (stratum corneum). Superbalm temporarily protects and helps relieve eczema, atopic dermatitis, rashes, itch, and irritation.

One sensitivity note: a 2,828-patient multicenter patch-test survey found only 7.2% of propolis-allergic subjects reacted to beeswax — cross-reactivity is rare, but if you have ragweed sensitivities, a patch test before first use is a reasonable precaution.

### Is beeswax safe for babies?

Beeswax (cera alba) is generally well-tolerated on infant skin. Unlike petrolatum, it has a semi-permeable structure built from long-chain esters that seals in moisture while still allowing the skin to breathe — an important distinction for sensitive baby skin. It works as an occlusive, reducing transepidermal water loss and forming a thin film on the skin's surface.

The main safety consideration is allergy. Beeswax can cross-react with propolis in sensitive individuals, though this is uncommon: a 2,828-patient patch-test survey found that only 7.2% of propolis-allergic subjects reacted to beeswax (Rajpara et al., 2009). For ragweed-sensitive families, a patch test before first use is a reasonable precaution.
