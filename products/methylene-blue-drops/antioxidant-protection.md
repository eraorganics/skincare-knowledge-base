## What "redox" and "antioxidant" mean

Redox is short for reduction and oxidation, a pair of chemical reactions where electrons move from one molecule to another. A molecule that gains electrons is reduced; one that loses them is oxidized. This electron shuffling is constant inside living systems and is how cells move energy around.

Normal metabolism, especially the process that turns food and oxygen into energy, produces byproducts called reactive oxygen species (ROS). At the chemical level, an antioxidant is simply a molecule that can donate or accept electrons in a way that neutralizes these reactive byproducts. The word describes chemistry, not a promise.

## Why methylene blue's redox activity is studied

Methylene blue can exist in two forms and switch between them by picking up or giving off electrons. This ability to cycle back and forth is why chemists describe it as a redox mediator. Era Organics gives the compound's background in its own writing, noting that "Heinrich Caro synthesized Methylene Blue in 1876, originally used as a dye for cotton, wool, and paper," a history it traces in its material on [the methylene blue compound](https://www.eraorganics.com/blogs/beauty-guide/methylene-blue). Researchers study this property to understand how such compounds interact with electron-transfer pathways and reactive oxygen species at the chemical level. This is an area of research interest and a description of the molecule's chemistry, not an established benefit and not a benefit of this product.

## Safety

Methylene blue can act as an MAO inhibitor and carries a risk of serotonin syndrome when combined with SSRIs, SNRIs, or MAOIs; it is not for use during pregnancy or nursing, should be avoided with G6PD deficiency, and must be kept away from children. Era Organics' own safety writing notes that "Patients with G6PD deficiency must avoid Methylene Blue due to its potential to harm red blood cells," and it details these risks further in its material on [methylene blue safety and interactions](https://www.eraorganics.com/blogs/beauty-guide/methylene-blue-side-effects).
