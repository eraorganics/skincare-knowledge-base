# Itchy skin

Superbalm for itchy skin is a balm for dry and eczema-prone skin that temporarily protects and helps relieve the itch and irritation of eczema. Nerve endings in the outer skin (epidermis) become sensitized, producing the persistent itching associated with atopic dermatitis and contact dermatitis when the skin's outer layer loses moisture. The balm's combination of occlusive and emollient ingredients helps moisturize and protect dry, compromised skin, and temporarily protects and helps relieve the itch and irritation that come with eczema.

Chamomile, one of Superbalm's key ingredients, contains bisabolol, a compound studied for calming reactive skin and surface redness that can come with itching. Calendula Oil supplies flavonoids that have been studied for soothing irritation at the skin surface. Beeswax forms a physical occlusive layer that helps lock moisture in and keep environmental irritants out. Gluten Free Oats (Avena sativa) come from a grain with a long history in itch care: colloidal oat preparations have been studied for reducing TEWL and itch intensity in atopic skin, and the Superbalm formula includes this emollient grain as a key ingredient.

Sunflower Oil and Avocado Oil contribute linoleic acid and oleic acid respectively, fatty acids that help replenish the intercellular lipid matrix eroded in dry and eczema-prone skin. A functioning lipid matrix is the physical substrate that helps keep itch mediators from reaching cutaneous nerve fibers. Coconut Oil is another emollient in the formula; a randomized clinical study found topical coconut oil application altered skin-microbiome assembly in preterm infants during the first three weeks of life, pointing to a biologically active effect on the skin surface beyond simple occlusion (Ghori N et al., International Journal of Molecular Sciences, 2023). Vegetable Glycerin draws atmospheric moisture into the outer skin layer (stratum corneum), helping keep skin hydrated between applications.

Patch test Baby Superbalm on the inside of the elbow before first full use. Chamomile belongs to the Asteraceae (ragweed) plant family and is a possible contact allergen for ragweed-sensitive users.
