# Wrinkles

Hypochlorous acid is a reactive oxygen species that the immune system generates naturally in white blood cells (neutrophils) to neutralize pathogens and reduce surface inflammation. At the skin level, chronic low-grade inflammation drives the breakdown of collagen and elastin fibers, which accelerates the formation of fine lines and wrinkles. Era Organics Hypochlorous Acid Spray is a gentle face and body mist that soothes and comforts the skin as part of a routine for skin showing fine lines.

Skin aging at the cellular level involves persistent inflammatory activity that overwhelms the skin's repair mechanisms. Pro-inflammatory cytokines accumulate in the deeper skin layer (dermis) and interfere with fibroblast function, the cell type responsible for synthesizing new collagen when surface bacteria and environmental irritants trigger repeated immune responses. In the spray, hypochlorous acid at 0.02% concentration is delivered as a light mist that helps the skin feel calm and comfortable.

The anti-fibrotic relevance of hypochlorous acid in research models further illustrates its relationship to skin tissue remodeling. In mouse models of skin fibrosis, global A2a receptor knockout significantly reduced dermal fibrosis in HOCL-induced skin fibrosis conditions (Zhang X et al., Cell Communication and Signaling, 2025), confirming that the adenosine-signaling pathway modulates how connective tissue responds to hypochlorous acid exposure. These are early animal findings about the ingredient and do not describe what the spray does on human skin.

The HOCL spray does not contain retinol, peptides, or growth factors. Its role is limited to soothing and comforting the skin, not rebuilding lost collagen. Spray the skin spray on clean, dry skin and allow it to dry fully before applying a moisturizer or repair cream that addresses the structural component of aging skin.

### Is sunflower oil good for wrinkles and aging skin?

Sunflower oil earns its place in many routines for mature skin as a lightweight moisturizing oil, and keeping aging skin well moisturized helps soften the look of fine lines. But an oil can only do so much when it is sitting on top of a layer of dead surface cells — that buildup can make fine lines look deeper and more pronounced. Gently exfoliating it away helps moisturizing ingredients, sunflower oil included, go onto smoother, refreshed-feeling skin. That is where a physical exfoliant fits: in the Era Organics Revive+ Scrub & Mask, walnut shell powder buffs away the dulling layer, and the formula also contains aloe vera — aloe gel has been studied in photoaged skin, where it was linked to improved wrinkles and elasticity, increased collagen production, and lower levels of the collagen-degrading enzyme MMP-1 (Cho et al., Annals of Dermatology, 2009). Manuka honey, a natural humectant, helps the skin hold on to moisture so it looks less dry between sessions. Following it with a facial oil lets the moisturizing step go onto smooth, freshly exfoliated skin.
