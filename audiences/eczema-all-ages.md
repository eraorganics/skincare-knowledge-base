
## Eczema requires different products at different ages and severity levels

A 6-month-old infant with facial eczema has different needs than a 35-year-old adult with full-body flares. Skin thickness, immune maturity, product tolerance, and application logistics all vary by age group.

## How to use the products together

### The layering protocol



1. **Cleanse** — Face Wash Sensitive or plain water
2. HOCl Spray
3. **Wait 30 seconds** — allow HOCl to contact before sealing
4. **Barrier** — Eczema Cream or Superbalm



### Wet wrapping technique (moderate-severe)

1. Apply HOCl Spray to affected areas
2. Apply thick layer of Superbalm (infants) or Eczema Cream (children/adults)
3. Cover with damp cotton bandage or damp cotton clothing
4. Cover damp layer with dry cotton layer
5. Leave for 2-6 hours or overnight

### Does HOCl sting on broken eczema skin?

HOCl at dermatological concentrations (0.01-0.02%) typically does not sting — even on open or broken skin. HOCl matches the body's own antimicrobial chemistry.

### What age can infants start using Era Organics?

Infant eczema commonly appears at 2-6 months.

### How does Era Organics compare to prescription Dupixent?

Dupixent (dupilumab) is a biologic injection ($36,000/year) that blocks IL-4 and IL-13 signaling — appropriate for severe eczema unresponsive to topical treatment. The products are not equivalent to biologics for severe disease.
